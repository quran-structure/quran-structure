# Research Dossier: The Ḥawāmīm Cluster (Surahs 40–46) — The Silk Brocades of the Qur'an (*Dībāj al-Qur'ān*)

## 1. Executive Summary & Epistemic Authority
The seven consecutive chapters opening with the disjointed letters **Ḥā-Mīm** (حم)—Surahs 40 through 46: **Ghāfir**, **Fuṣṣilat**, **Ash-Shūrā**, **Az-Zukhruf**, **Ad-Dukhān**, **Al-Jāthiyah**, and **Al-Aḥqāf**—constitute the most tightly integrated, rhetorically unified family of surahs in the entire ʿUthmānic codex.

In classical Sunni literature, this heptad is celebrated through authentic Sahabah traditions:
- **Ibn Masʿūd (رضي الله عنه):**
  > «إِذَا وَقَعْتُ فِي آلِ حم وَقَعْتُ فِي رَوْضَاتٍ أَتَأَنَّقُ فِيهِنَّ»  
  > *"When I enter the family of Ḥā Mīm, I enter lush, fragrant meadows in which I delight."* (Reported by Al-Bukhārī in his *Ṣaḥīḥ*, Kitāb Faḍā'il al-Qur'ān, and Al-Bayhaqī in *Shuʿab al-Īmān*).
- **Ibn ʿAbbās (رضي الله عنهما):**
  > «إِنَّ لِكُلِّ شَيْءٍ لُبَابًا، وَإِنَّ لُبَابَ الْقُرْآنِ آلُ حم»  
  > *"Everything possesses an essence or marrow, and the essence of the Qur'an is the family of Ḥā Mīm."* (Recorded by Al-Dārimī and cited by Al-Suyūṭī in *Al-Itqān*).
- **Canonical Moniker:**
  > «حَم دِيبَاجُ الْقُرْآنِ»  
  > *"The Ḥā Mīm surahs are the silk brocades (the royal bridal garments) of the Qur'an."* (Cited by Al-Zarkashī in *Al-Burhān* and Al-Suyūṭī in *Tanāsuq al-Durar*).

While sharing structural markers—the majestic descent of the Book (*Tanzīl al-Kitāb*), confrontation with dialectical arrogance (*jadal / istikbār*), cosmic signposts (*āyāt al-āfāq wa-l-anfus*), and divine sovereignty—each of the seven surahs preserves an unmistakable, singular personality (*Jaww al-Sūrah*), contributing a vital, irreplaceable voice to the grand theological symphony.

## 2. Distinct Personalities Across the Heptad

| Surah | Classical Name | Distinctive Voice | Core Personality (*Jaww al-Sūrah*) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **40. Ghāfir** | *Al-Mu'min* | Courageous, pleading, sovereign | The courtroom hero (*Mu'min Āli Firʿawn*) who risks his life for truth, framed by the cosmic solidarity of the Throne-bearing angels and the supreme inquest: *Li-manil-mulku al-yawm?* |
| **41. Fuṣṣilat** | *Ḥā-Mīm As-Sajdah* | Eloquent, forensic, visceral | The expounded Arabic manifest, the cosmic creation of heavens and earth in willing obedience, and the terrifying day when human ears, eyes, and skin testify against their owners. |
| **42. Ash-Shūrā** | *Ḥā-Mīm ʿAyn-Sīn-Qāf* | Ecumenical, unifying, consultative | The central five-letter hinge linking all prophets under one eternal law: *Aqīmud-dīn*; communal governance through consultation (*Shūrā*), and the transcendent modes of divine communication. |
| **43. Az-Zukhruf** | *The Ornaments of Gold* | Deconstructive, satirical, elevated | The exposure of materialistic meritocracy and gilded delusions (*zukhruf*), contrasting transient worldly wealth against the celestial Archetype (*Umm al-Kitāb*). |
| **44. Ad-Dukhān** | *The Visible Smoke* | Cinematic, urgent, ominous | The nocturnal descent of wisdom in *Laylatun Mubārakah* sharply contrasted against the choking, apocalyptic smoke that suffocates arrogant imperial powers. |
| **45. Al-Jāthiyah** | *The Kneeling / Crouching* | Judicial, philosophical, humbling | The dismantling of secular naturalism (*Ad-Dahriyyah*) and personal whim (*hawā*), culminating in the awe-inspiring sight of all human nations fallen on their knees before the Divine Ledger. |
| **46. Al-Aḥqāf** | *The Sand Dunes* | Haunting, steadfast, filial | The vanished superpower of ʿĀd buried beneath the desert sands, the tender sanctity of parental sacrifice, the unseen listening jinn, and the heroic seal of apostolic patience (*Ulu al-ʿAzm*). |

## 3. Sequence of Munāsabāt & Transitions

### A. The Grand Portal: Sūrat Az-Zumar (39) $\rightarrow$ Sūrat Ghāfir (40)
* **Closing Verse of Az-Zumar (39:75):**
  > «وَتَرَى ٱلۡمَلَٰٓئِكَةَ حَآفِّينَ مِنۡ حَوۡلِ ٱلۡعَرۡشِ يُسَبِّحُونَ بِحَمۡدِ رَبِّهِمۡۚ وَقُضِيَ بَيۡنَهُم بِٱلۡحَقِّۚ وَقِيلَ ٱلۡحَمۡدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ ٱلۡعَٰلَمِينَ»
  > *"And you will see the angels surrounding the Throne, exalting [Allah] with praise of their Lord. And it will be judged between them in truth, and it will be said, '[All] praise to Allah, Lord of the worlds.'"*
* **Opening Verses of Ghāfir (40:1–3, 7):**
  > «حمٓ . تَنزِيلُ ٱلۡكِتَٰبِ مِنَ ٱللَّهِ ٱلۡعَزِيزِ ٱلۡعَلِيمِ . غَافِرِ ٱلذَّنۢبِ وَقَابِلِ ٱلتَّوۡبِ شَدِيدِ ٱلۡعِقَابِ ذِي ٱلطَّوۡلِۖ لَآ إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَۖ إِلَيۡهِ ٱلۡمَصِيرُ»
  > *"Ḥā Mīm. The revelation of the Book is from Allah, the Exalted in Might, the Knowing. The forgiver of sin, acceptor of repentance, severe in punishment, owner of abundance. There is no deity except Him; to Him is the destination."*
  > 
  > And in 40:7:
  > «ٱلَّذِينَ يَحۡمِلُونَ ٱلۡعَرۡشَ وَمَنۡ حَوۡلَهُۥ يُسَبِّحُونَ بِحَمۡدِ رَبِّهِمۡ وَيُؤۡمِنُونَ بِهِۦ وَيَسۡتَغۡفِرُونَ لِلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُواْ...»
  > *"Those [angels] who carry the Throne and those around it exalt [Allah] with praise of their Lord and believe in Him and ask forgiveness for those who have believed..."*
* **Classical Munāsabah Analysis:**
  * **Al-Suyūṭī (*Tanāsuq al-Durar*):** Observes that Az-Zumar concludes with the ultimate eschatological picture: judgment has passed, and the celestial angels encircle the Divine Throne (*ḥāffīna min ḥawli al-ʿarsh*), magnifying their Lord with praise. Surah Ghāfir immediately opens by revealing what those very Throne-encircling and Throne-bearing angels are doing during earthly time: they are ceaselessly supplicating for the forgiveness and salvation of the fragile believers on earth!
  * **Al-Biqāʿī (*Naẓm al-Durar*):** Points out that Az-Zumar ended with *«wa-qīla al-ḥamdu lillāhi rabbi al-ʿālamīn»*, and Ghāfir immediately establishes the divine attributes that warrant this praise—the exquisite balance of mercy and justice: *Ghāfir al-dhanb* (covering sins), *Qābil al-tawb* (embracing return), *Shadīd al-ʿiqāb* (deterring oppressors), and *Dhī al-ṭawl* (overflowing grace).

### B. Transition 1: Sūrat Ghāfir (40) $\rightarrow$ Sūrat Fuṣṣilat (41)
* **Closing Verse of Ghāfir (40:85):**
  > «فَلَمۡ يَكُ يَنفَعُهُمۡ إِيمَٰنُهُمۡ لَمَّا رَأَوۡاْ بَأۡسَنَاۖ سُنَّتَ ٱللَّهِ ٱلَّتِي قَدۡ خَلَتۡ فِي عِبَادِهِۦۖ وَخَسِرَ هُنَالِكَ ٱلۡكَٰفِرُونَ»
  > *"But never did their faith benefit them once they saw Our punishment. [It is] the established way of Allah which has preceded among His servants. And the disbelievers thereupon lost [all]."*
* **Opening Verses of Fuṣṣilat (41:1–4):**
  > «حمٓ . تَنزِيلࣱ مِّنَ ٱلرَّحۡمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ . كِتَٰبࣱ فُصِّلَتۡ ءَايَٰتُهُۥ قُرۡءَانًا عَرَبِيࣰّا لِّقَوۡمࣲ يَعۡلَمُونَ . بَشِيرࣰا وَنَذِيرࣰا فَأَعۡرَضَ أَكۡثَرُهُمۡ فَهُمۡ لَا يَسۡمَعُونَ»
  > *"Ḥā Mīm. [This is] a revelation from the Entirely Merciful, the Especially Merciful — a Book whose verses have been detailed, an Arabic Qur'an for a people who know, as a bearer of good tidings and a warner; but most of them turned away, so they do not hear."*
* **Classical Munāsabah Analysis:**
  * **Ibn al-Zubayr al-Gharnāṭī (*Al-Burhān*):** Ghāfir demonstrated the tragic tragedy of post-facto faith: when tyrants witness the catastrophic wrath of Allah (*lammā ra'aw ba'sanā*), their sudden faith is completely void and useless according to Allah's immutable Sunnah. To prevent humans from falling into this irreversible trap, Fuṣṣilat immediately opens by proclaiming a detailed, perfectly expounded revelation (*kitābun fuṣṣilat āyātuhū*) sent beforehand from the *Raḥmān* and *Raḥīm*, offering ample warning and guidance while time remains.
  * **Al-Biqāʿī (*Naẓm al-Durar*):** Ghāfir dealt with contentious argument against the revelation (*mā yujādilu fī āyātillāh illalladhīna kafarū*). Fuṣṣilat demonstrates that this disputation does not arise from any obscurity in the message, but from willful sensory refusal: *«fa-aʿraḍa aktharuhum fa-hum lā yasmaʿūn»*.

---

### C. Transition 2: Sūrat Fuṣṣilat (41) $\rightarrow$ Sūrat Ash-Shūrā (42)
* **Closing Verses of Fuṣṣilat (41:53–54):**
  > «سَنُرِيهِمۡ ءَايَٰتِنَا فِي ٱلۡأٓفَاقِ وَفِيٓ أَنفُسِهِمۡ حَتَّىٰ يَتَبَيَّنَ لَهُمۡ أَنَّهُ ٱلۡحَقُّۗ أَوَلَمۡ يَكۡفِ بِرَبِّكَ أَنَّهُۥ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَيۡءࣲ شَهِيدٌ . أَلَآ إِنَّهُمۡ فِي مِرۡيَةࣲ مِّن لِّقَآءِ رَبِّهِمۡۗ أَلَآ إِنَّهُۥ بِكُلِّ شَيۡءࣲ مُّحِيطُۢ»
  > *"We will show them Our signs in the horizons and within themselves until it becomes clear to them that it is the truth. But is it not sufficient concerning your Lord that He is, over all things, a Witness? Unquestionably, they are in doubt about the meeting with their Lord. Unquestionably He is, of all things, encompassing."*
* **Opening Verses of Ash-Shūrā (42:1–3):**
  > «حمٓ . عٓسٓقٓ . كَذَٰلِكَ يُوحِيٓ إِلَيۡكَ وَإِلَى ٱلَّذِينَ مِن قَبۡلِكَ ٱللَّهُ ٱلۡعَزِيزُ ٱلۡحَكِيمُ»
  > *"Ḥā Mīm. ʿAyn Sīn Qāf. Thus has He revealed to you, [O Muhammad], and to those before you — Allah, the Exalted in Might, the Wise."*
* **Classical Munāsabah Analysis:**
  * **Al-Suyūṭī (*Tanāsuq al-Durar*):** Fuṣṣilat closes with the cosmic and internal arenas of revelation: Allah promises to display His signs throughout the horizons of the universe (*al-āfāq*) and within the human psyche (*fī anfusihim*). Ash-Shūrā takes this cosmic truth and anchors it in the historical, longitudinal stream of divine prophecy: *«Kadhālika yūḥī ilayka wa-ilā alladhīna min qablika»*. The revelation given to Muhammad (ﷺ) is not an isolated anomaly, but the identical divine light revealed to all previous messengers.
  * **The Five-Letter Central Pivot:** Ash-Shūrā stands as the literal midpoint and structural hinge of the seven Ḥawāmīm. While the other six surahs open with two letters (حم), Ash-Shūrā expands to five letters (حم . عسق), mirroring the universal comprehensiveness of its ecumenical scope (*Sharāʿa lakum min al-dīni mā waṣṣā bihī Nūḥan...* [42:13]).

---

### D. Transition 3: Sūrat Ash-Shūrā (42) $\rightarrow$ Sūrat Az-Zukhruf (43)
* **Closing Verses of Ash-Shūrā (42:52–53):**
  > «وَكَذَٰلِكَ أَوۡحَيۡنَآ إِلَيۡكَ رُوحࣰا مِّنۡ أَمۡرِنَاۚ مَا كُنتَ تَدۡرِي مَا ٱلۡكِتَٰبُ وَلَا ٱلۡإِيمَٰنُ وَلَٰكِن جَعَلۡنَٰهُ نُورࣰا نَّهۡدِي بِهِۦ مَن نَّشَآءُ مِنۡ عِبَادِنَاۚ وَإِنَّكَ لَتَهۡدِيٓ إِلَىٰ صِرَٰطࣲ مُّسۡتَقِيمࣲ . صِرَٰطِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلَّذِي لَهُۥ مَا فِي ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَمَا فِي ٱلۡأَرۡضِۗ أَلَآ إِلَى ٱللَّهِ تَصِيرُ ٱلۡأُمُورُ»
  > *"And thus We have revealed to you an inspiration of Our command. You did not know what is the Book or [what is] faith, but We have made it a light by which We guide whom We will of Our servants. And indeed, [O Muhammad], you guide to a straight path — the path of Allah, to whom belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth. Unquestionably, to Allah do [all] matters return."*
* **Opening Verses of Az-Zukhruf (43:1–4):**
  > «حمٓ . وَٱلۡكِتَٰبِ ٱلۡمُبِينِ . إِنَّا جَعَلۡنَٰهُ قُرۡءَٰنًا عَرَبِيࣰّا لَّعَلَّكُمۡ تَعۡقِلُونَ . وَإِنَّهُۥ فِيٓ أُمِّ ٱلۡكِتَٰبِ لَدَيۡنَا لَعَلِيٌّ حَكِيمٌ»
  > *"Ḥā Mīm. By the clear Book, indeed, We have made it an Arabic Qur'an that you might understand. And indeed, it is — in the Mother of the Book with Us — exalted and full of wisdom."*
* **Classical Munāsabah Analysis:**
  * **Ibn al-Zubayr al-Gharnāṭī (*Al-Burhān*):** Ash-Shūrā concludes by defining the nature of the revelation: it is a *Rūḥ* (life-giving spirit) and a *Nūr* (illuminating light) that guides to the *Ṣirāṭ Mustaqīm*. Az-Zukhruf opens with a solemn divine oath swearing by that very entity: *«Wal-Kitābi al-Mubīn»*, and traces its transcendent ontological origin to the *Umm al-Kitāb* (the Preserved Tablet with Allah).
  * **Thematic Contrast of Substance vs. Superficiality:** Ash-Shūrā ended with the supreme dignity of the Prophet guiding humanity. Az-Zukhruf addresses the shallow, materialistic objection of the Quraysh: *«Lawlā nuzzila hādhā al-qur'ānu ʿalā rajulin min al-qaryatayni ʿaẓīm!»* (Why was this Qur'an not sent down to an elite billionaire of Mecca or Ta'if?). Az-Zukhruf deconstructs their delusion, showing that worldly ornaments and palaces of gold (*zukhruf*) are trivial trinkets that Allah gives even to disbelievers, whereas the celestial Book is eternal.

---

### E. Transition 4: Sūrat Az-Zukhruf (43) $\rightarrow$ Sūrat Ad-Dukhān (44)
* **Closing Verses of Az-Zukhruf (43:88–89):**
  > «وَقِيلِهِۦ يَٰرَبِّ إِنَّ هَٰٓؤُلَآءِ قَوۡمࣱ لَّا يُؤۡمِنُونَ . فَٱصۡفَحۡ عَنۡهُمۡ وَقُلۡ سَلَٰمࣱۚ فَسَوۡفَ يَعۡلَمُونَ»
  > *"And [Allah acknowledges] his saying, 'O my Lord, indeed these are a people who do not believe.' So turn aside from them and say, 'Peace.' But they are going to know."*
* **Opening Verses of Ad-Dukhān (44:1–4, 10):**
  > «حمٓ . وَٱلۡكِتَٰبِ ٱلۡمُبِينِ . إِنَّآ أَنزَلۡنَٰهُ فِي لَيۡلَةࣲ مُّبَٰرَكَةٍۚ إِنَّا كُنَّا مُنذِرِينَ . فِيهَا يُفۡرَقُ كُلُّ أَمۡرٍ حَكِيمٍ»
  > *"Ḥā Mīm. By the clear Book, indeed, We sent it down during a blessed night. Indeed, We were to warn [mankind]. Therein is made distinct every matter of wisdom..."*
  > 
  > And in 44:10:
  > «فَٱرۡتَقِبۡ يَوۡمَ تَأۡتِي ٱلسَّمَآءُ بِدُخَانࣲ مُّبِينࣲ»
  > *"Then watch for the Day when the sky will bring a visible smoke."*
* **Classical Munāsabah Analysis:**
  * **Al-Suyūṭī (*Tanāsuq al-Durar*):** Az-Zukhruf closes with the Prophet's agonizing, heartbreaking grievance presented to the Lord: *«Yā Rabbi inna hā'ulā'i qawmun lā yu'minūn»*, to which Allah issues the resolute decree: *«Fa-ṣfaḥ ʿanhum wa-qul salām; fa-sawfa yaʿlamūn»* ("Pardon them and say peace; for they will soon know!"). Ad-Dukhān immediately reveals the exact, catastrophic nature of what they will know: *«Fartaqib yawma ta'tī al-samā'u bi-dukhānin mubīn»* ("Then watch for the day when the sky will bring a visible smoke that envelops the people!").
  * **Al-Biqāʿī (*Naẓm al-Durar*):** Points out the paired openings: both Az-Zukhruf and Ad-Dukhān share the identical oath: *«Ḥā-Mīm . Wal-Kitābi al-Mubīn»*. Az-Zukhruf emphasizes the Book's loftiness in *Umm al-Kitāb*, while Ad-Dukhān details the earthly temporal gateway of its historical descent during *Laylatun Mubārakah* (The Night of Decree).

---

### F. Transition 5: Sūrat Ad-Dukhān (44) $\rightarrow$ Sūrat Al-Jāthiyah (45)
* **Closing Verses of Ad-Dukhān (44:58–59):**
  > «فَإِنَّمَا يَسَّرۡنَٰهُ بِلِسَانِكَ لَعَلَّهُمۡ يَتَذَكَّرُونَ . فَٱرۡتَقِبۡ إِنَّهُم مُّرۡتَقِبُونَ»
  > *"And indeed, We have eased the Qur'an in your tongue that they might be reminded. So watch, [O Muhammad]; indeed, they are watching."*
* **Opening Verses of Al-Jāthiyah (45:1–3):**
  > «حمٓ . تَنزِيلُ ٱلۡكِتَٰبِ مِنَ ٱللَّهِ ٱلۡعَزِيزِ ٱلۡحَكِيمِ . إِنَّ فِي ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلۡأَرۡضِ لَأٓيَٰتࣲ لِّلۡمُؤۡمِنِينَ»
  > *"Ḥā Mīm. The revelation of the Book is from Allah, the Exalted in Might, the Wise. Indeed, within the heavens and earth are signs for the believers."*
* **Classical Munāsabah Analysis:**
  * **Al-Biqāʿī (*Naẓm al-Durar*):** Ad-Dukhān concludes with the stance of mutual anticipation: *«Fartaqib innahum murtaqibūn»* ("Watch and wait; they too are waiting"). Al-Jāthiyah opens by presenting the definitive empirical proofs embedded in the cosmos that justify why the believer waits with certainty while the denier waits in peril.
  * **Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī (*Mafātīḥ al-Ghayb*):** Ad-Dukhān depicted Pharaoh and his hosts drowning, followed by the striking elegy: *«Fa-mā bakat ʿalayhim al-samā'u wal-arḍ»* ("Neither the heavens nor the earth wept over them"). Al-Jāthiyah opens by turning our gaze directly back to those very silent witnesses: *«Inna fī al-samāwāti wal-arḍi la-āyātin lil-mu'minīn»*. The cosmos that had no tears for tyrants is overflowing with signs for those who reflect. This culminates in verse 28, where all waiting ends: *«Wa-tarā kulla ummatin jāthiyah»* ("And you will see every nation fallen on its knees").

---

### G. Transition 6: Sūrat Al-Jāthiyah (45) $\rightarrow$ Sūrat Al-Aḥqāf (46)
* **Closing Verses of Al-Jāthiyah (45:36–37):**
  > «فَلِلَّهِ ٱلۡحَمۡدُ رَبِّ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَرَبِّ ٱلۡأَرۡضِ رَبِّ ٱلۡعَٰلَمِينَ . وَلَهُ ٱلۡكِبۡرِيَآءُ فِي ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلۡأَرۡضِۖ وَهُوَ ٱلۡعَزِيزُ ٱلۡحَكِيمُ»
  > *"Then, to Allah belongs [all] praise — Lord of the heavens and Lord of the earth, Lord of the worlds. And to Him belongs [all] grandeur within the heavens and the earth, and He is the Exalted in Might, the Wise."*
* **Opening Verses of Al-Aḥqāf (46:1–3):**
  > «حمٓ . تَنزِيلُ ٱلۡكِتَٰبِ مِنَ ٱللَّهِ ٱلۡعَزِيزِ ٱلۡحَكِيمِ . مَا خَلَقۡنَا ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلۡأَرۡضَ وَمَا بَيۡنَهُمَآ إِلَّا بِٱلۡحَقِّ وَأَجَلࣲ مُّسَمࣰّىۚ وَٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُواْ عَمَّآ أُنذِرُواْ مُعۡرِضُونَ»
  > *"Ḥā Mīm. The revelation of the Book is from Allah, the Exalted in Might, the Wise. We did not create the heavens and earth and what is between them except in truth and [for] a specified term. But those who disbelieve, from that of which they are warned, are turning away."*
* **Classical Munāsabah Analysis:**
  * **Al-Suyūṭī (*Tanāsuq al-Durar*):** Al-Jāthiyah closes by attributing absolute praise (*Al-Ḥamd*) and absolute supremacy/majesty (*Al-Kibriyā'*) in the heavens and earth to Allah, concluding with His twin attributes: *«Wa-huwa al-ʿAzīzu al-Ḥakīm»*. Al-Aḥqāf immediately inherits this identical divine pairing at its outset: *«Tanzīlu al-Kitābi min Allāhi al-ʿAzīzi al-Ḥakīm»*, and applies His wisdom to the cosmic purpose of creation: *«Mā khalaqnā al-samāwāti wal-arḍa wa-mā baynahumā illā bil-ḥaqqi wa-ajalin musammā»*.
  * **The Heptad's Grand Conclusion:** Al-Aḥqāf serves as the summary capstone of the entire Ḥawāmīm family. It gathers all previous threads: the historical ruins of ancient superpowers (the mighty people of ʿĀd in the sand dunes, echoing Pharaoh in Ghāfir), the reality of resurrection, the confirmation of the Mosaic scripture, and the unseen response of the Jinn who heard this expounded Arabic Quran and immediately submitted.

### H. The Exit from the Ḥawāmīm: Sūrat Al-Aḥqāf (46) $\rightarrow$ Sūrat Muḥammad (47)
* **Closing Verse of Al-Aḥqāf (46:35):**
  > «فَٱصۡبِرۡ كَمَا صَبَرَ أُوْلُواْ ٱلۡعَزۡمِ مِنَ ٱلرُّسُلِ وَلَا تَسۡتَعۡجِل لَّهُمۡۚ كَأَنَّهُمۡ يَوۡمَ يَرَوۡنَ مَا يُوعَدُونَ لَمۡ يَلۡبَثُوٓاْ إِلَّا سَاعَةࣰ مِّن نَّهَارِۭۚ بَلَٰغࣱۚ فَهَلۡ يُهۡلَكُ إِلَّا ٱلۡقَوۡمُ ٱلۡفَٰسِقُونَ»
  > *"So be patient, [O Muhammad], as were those of determination among the messengers and do not be impatient for them. It will be — on the Day they see that which they are promised — as though they had not remained [in the world] except an hour of a day. [This is] notification. And will [any] be destroyed except the defiantly disobedient people?"*
* **Opening Verses of Sūrat Muḥammad (47:1–2):**
  > «ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُواْ وَصَدُّواْ عَن سَبِيلِ ٱللَّهِ أَضَلَّ أَعۡمَٰلَهُمۡ . وَٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُواْ وَعَمِلُواْ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ وَءَامَنُواْ بِمَا نُزِّلَ عَلَىٰ مُحَمَّدࣲ وَهُوَ ٱلۡحَقُّ مِن رَّبِّهِمۡ كَفَّرَ عَنۡهُمۡ سَيِّـَٔاتِهِمۡ وَأَصۡلَحَ بَالَهُمۡ»
  > *"Those who disbelieve and avert [people] from the way of Allah — He will waste their deeds. And those who believe and do righteous deeds and believe in what has been sent down upon Muhammad — and it is the truth from their Lord — He will remove from them their misdeeds and amend their condition."*
* **Classical Munāsabah Analysis:**
  * **Al-Biqāʿī (*Naẓm al-Durar*):** Al-Aḥqāf ends with the ultimate directive of Makkan patience addressed to the Prophet: *«Fa-ṣbir kamā ṣabara ulu al-ʿazmi min al-rusul»*. Sūrat Muḥammad (the opening of the subsequent Medinan block, also named *Sūrat al-Qitāl*) names the Messenger explicitly: *«wa-āmanū bi-mā nuzzila ʿalā Muḥammad»*. The era of passive endurance now transitions into active divine defense, where Allah systematically voids the plots of the persecutors (*aḍalla aʿmālahum*) while establishing and rectifying the affairs of the believers (*aṣlaḥa bālahum*).
  * **Al-Suyūṭī (*Tanāsuq al-Durar*):** Points out the symmetrical balance: Al-Aḥqāf asks rhetorically at its end: *«Fa-hal yuhlaku illā al-qawmu al-fāsiqūn?»* ("Will any be destroyed except the defiantly disobedient?"). Sūrat Muḥammad begins by detailing the exact spiritual destruction of those very disobedient: their life's efforts are thoroughly nullified and lost!
