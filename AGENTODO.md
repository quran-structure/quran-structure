# Agent TODO --- Qur'anic Surah Relationships, Munāsabāt & Structural Research

**Project type:** Deep research + source-backed knowledge base +
interactive website\
**Primary research tradition:** Sunni Qur'anic scholarship\
**Working title:** *The Qur'an in Conversation: A Study of Surah, Ayah
and Thematic Connections*\
**Primary language:** English\
**Arabic:** Preserve Qur'anic Arabic accurately; provide reliable
translations and transliteration only where useful for technical terms.\
**Research rule:** Never present a proposed connection as certain unless
the evidence warrants that level of confidence.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. Mission

Build a rigorous, beautiful, navigable research website that
investigates how the 114 surahs and their ayat relate to one another
within the canonical muṣḥaf order.

The project studies:

1.  **Munāsabāt al-suwar** --- relationships between surahs, especially
    consecutive surahs.
2.  **Munāsabāt al-āyāt** --- relationships between adjacent or
    non-adjacent verses.
3.  **Surah architecture** --- internal structure, thematic sections,
    transitions, openings, endings, refrains and pivots.
4.  **Thematic networks** --- how recurring Qur'anic themes, narratives,
    vocabulary, divine names, promises and warnings connect across the
    Qur'an.
5.  **Thematic development** --- how a subject is introduced, expanded,
    contrasted, answered or brought to a climax across multiple surahs.
6.  **Canonical sequence** --- what can responsibly be said about the
    placement of a surah in the muṣḥaf, without confusing canonical
    order with chronological order of revelation.

The website must answer questions such as:

-   Why does Al-Fātiḥah precede Al-Baqarah? How does the request for
    guidance in Al-Fātiḥah relate to the Qur'an being guidance in
    Al-Baqarah 2:2?
-   How do Al-Baqarah and Āl ʿImrān relate in subject, argument,
    audience, covenant, revelation and response to earlier communities?
-   What literary, theological and thematic connections may be
    investigated across Al-Qamar (54), Ar-Raḥmān (55) and Al-Wāqiʿah
    (56)?
-   How does the repeated refrain in Ar-Raḥmān function, and how does
    its subject matter compare with the signs, warnings and resurrection
    scenes in Al-Qamar and Al-Wāqiʿah?
-   How do a surah's opening and ending correspond?
-   Where does a theme recur elsewhere, and does the later passage
    develop the same point or address a different context?

This is not a project to manufacture hidden codes, numerical miracles or
speculative links. Its purpose is careful tadabbur supported by Qur'anic
text, recognised tafsir, Qur'anic sciences and transparent reasoning.

## 2. Non-negotiable research principles

### 2.1 Sunni source discipline

Use recognised Sunni tafsir and ʿulūm al-Qur'an works as the primary
interpretive foundation. Prioritise:

-   Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī --- *Jāmiʿ al-bayān*
-   Tafsīr al-Baghawī --- *Maʿālim al-tanzīl*
-   Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr --- *Tafsīr al-Qur'ān al-ʿaẓīm*
-   Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī --- *Al-Jāmiʿ li-aḥkām al-Qur'ān*
-   Tafsīr al-Rāzī --- *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb* (evaluate arguments carefully)
-   Tafsīr Ibn ʿĀshūr --- *Al-Taḥrīr wa al-tanwīr*
-   Al-Zarkashī --- *Al-Burhān fī ʿulūm al-Qur'ān*
-   Al-Suyūṭī --- *Al-Itqān fī ʿulūm al-Qur'ān*
-   Al-Biqāʿī --- *Naẓm al-durar fī tanāsub al-āyāt wa al-suwar*
-   Ibn al-Zubayr al-Gharnāṭī --- *Al-Burhān fī tartīb suwar al-Qur'ān*

Use primary texts whenever available. Record the edition, volume, page,
chapter or tafsir location. Page numbers vary by edition; do not invent
them.

The project may consult reputable academic studies for literary
analysis, textual structure and research history. Label these as
**secondary academic research**, not as Sunni tafsir or religious
authority. Do not let an academic theory silently override Qur'anic text
or recognised tafsir.

Do not present sectarian polemics, unattributed internet claims,
social-media posts or anonymous blogs as evidence. Do not use Shia,
Qadiani/Ahmadi or other non-Sunni doctrinal sources as authorities for
the project's Sunni interpretive conclusions. If a non-Sunni or secular
academic work is relevant to a historiographical question, it must be
clearly identified, contextualised and kept separate from the Sunni
tafsir evidence.

### 2.2 Evidence hierarchy

For each claim, use this order of evaluation:

1.  Qur'anic Arabic text and immediate context.
2.  Strong, relevant hadith reports, with source and grading/assessment
    where available.
3.  Statements and explanations from recognised early tafsir
    authorities.
4.  Classical Sunni works on munāsabāt and ʿulūm al-Qur'an.
5.  Later recognised Sunni tafsir, including linguistic and rhetorical
    analysis.
6.  Reputable contemporary scholarship, clearly labelled.
7.  Research team's own comparative observation, explicitly labelled as
    an observation or hypothesis.

A claim does not become established merely because it appears in a
modern article or because two passages share a word.

### 2.3 Confidence labels

Every relationship must carry one of these labels:

-   **Explicitly stated by a classical mufassir:** A named scholar
    directly explains the relationship. Quote or accurately paraphrase
    and cite the exact location.
-   **Supported by multiple tafsir sources:** More than one recognised
    source supports the connection.
-   **Classical scholarly observation:** A named scholar proposes or
    discusses the connection, but it is not necessarily universally
    accepted.
-   **Textually observable:** A clear textual feature exists (shared
    wording, refrain, contrast, repeated narrative, adjacent
    transition), but its interpretive significance needs analysis.
-   **Research hypothesis:** A plausible connection proposed by this
    project, requiring further review.
-   **Weak / not established:** Insufficient evidence. Do not promote it
    as a conclusion.

Do not use numerical confidence percentages unless a documented scoring
method is created and validated. Prefer the labels above.

### 2.4 No forced coherence

-   Do not assume every pair of surahs has one simple, exclusive theme
    linking them.
-   Do not claim that a connection is the reason for canonical placement
    unless a reliable source explicitly supports that claim or the
    conclusion is clearly presented as an inference.
-   Distinguish a **textual relationship** from a claim about **divine
    intent**.
-   Similar subject matter does not automatically prove direct literary
    dependence or a deliberate cross-reference.
-   Record alternative interpretations and scholarly disagreement.
-   State when no strong relationship has been established.
-   Never fabricate Arabic wording, verse numbers, hadith, quotations,
    tafsir citations or bibliographic details.

## 3. Core research questions and analytical framework

For every surah, investigate the following dimensions.

### A. Surah identity and context

-   Surah number and recognised name(s).
-   Makki/Madani classification, with source and any disagreement.
-   Number of ayat, noting the counting convention if relevant.
-   Relevant authentic reports concerning virtues or occasions of
    revelation.
-   Main subjects and central argument, without reducing the surah to
    one slogan.
-   Audience, discourse setting and major changes in addressee.

### B. Internal architecture

Identify plausible thematic units and transitions. For each unit record:

-   Starting and ending ayah.
-   A concise descriptive heading (editorial heading, not part of the
    Qur'an).
-   Main subject and sub-themes.
-   Speaker/addressee changes.
-   Argument or narrative progression.
-   Repeated words, phrases and refrains.
-   Contrast, parallelism, question-answer patterns and shifts in rhythm
    or rhetorical mode.
-   Relationship to the surah's opening, central movement and ending.
-   Evidence and confidence label.

Do not force symmetrical structures, ring compositions or chiasms. If a
proposed structure is disputed, show the competing segmentations and
explain the evidence.

### C. Opening and ending

Investigate:

-   How does the opening establish the subject, question, warning,
    praise or instruction?
-   How does the ending return to, answer, complete, intensify or
    contrast with the opening?
-   Does the final passage prepare for the next surah?
-   Are these links discussed by al-Suyūṭī, al-Zarkashī, al-Biqāʿī, Ibn
    ʿĀshūr or other recognised scholars?

### D. Relationship to neighbouring surahs

For each surah, study:

-   Previous surah → current surah.
-   Current surah → next surah.
-   Shared themes and vocabulary.
-   Complementary or contrasting arguments.
-   Narrative or doctrinal progression.
-   Opening-to-ending transitions.
-   Classical munāsabah explanations.
-   Alternative explanations and confidence level.

Do not limit the study to adjacency. Also map meaningful non-adjacent
links, but explain why they matter.

### E. Cross-Qur'anic thematic relationships

Build thematic networks for topics including (not limited to):

-   Tawḥīd and Allah's names and attributes.
-   Revelation, the Qur'an and guidance.
-   Prophethood and messengership.
-   Creation, the heavens and the earth.
-   Signs in nature and human life.
-   Human origin, purpose and responsibility.
-   Gratitude, blessings and ingratitude.
-   Faith, hypocrisy and disbelief.
-   Earlier prophets and communities.
-   Covenants and the Children of Israel.
-   Justice, mercy, repentance and forgiveness.
-   Worship, prayer, fasting, charity and pilgrimage.
-   Death, resurrection, the Last Day and accountability.
-   Paradise, Hell and the states of their inhabitants.
-   Patience, reliance, steadfastness and duʿā'.

For each theme, distinguish shared vocabulary from shared meaning.
Record where contexts differ.

## 4. Required pilot investigations

Complete these pilot studies before scaling to all 114 surahs. They will
validate the research method and website design.

### Pilot 1 --- Al-Fātiḥah (1) → Al-Baqarah (2)

Primary question: How does the request for guidance in Al-Fātiḥah relate
to the description of the Book as guidance in Al-Baqarah 2:2?

Investigate:

-   The movement from praise, worship and supplication to the
    answer/description of guidance.
-   The relationship between *ihdinā al-ṣirāṭ al-mustaqīm* (1:6) and
    *hudan lil-muttaqīn* (2:2).
-   The contrast between those guided, those who incur anger and those
    who go astray (1:7), and the categories and responses described
    early in Al-Baqarah.
-   Whether classical scholars explicitly identify this relationship.
-   How Al-Baqarah develops the guidance theme through belief,
    revelation, human responsibility, the stories of Adam and the
    Children of Israel, and the community's obligations.

Deliver a verse-grounded explanation, not merely the claim that
Al-Baqarah is the answer to Al-Fātiḥah.

### Pilot 2 --- Al-Baqarah (2) → Āl ʿImrān (3)

Investigate:

-   Shared discussion of revelation, scripture, guidance and earlier
    communities.
-   The relationship between the treatment of the Children of Israel and
    the discussion of the People of the Book.
-   Abraham, the House, covenant, worship and the formation of the
    Muslim community.
-   The movement from community formation and law to steadfastness,
    trials, unity and reliance upon Allah.
-   Connections between the endings and openings of the two surahs.
-   Classical explanations, including where available the analyses of
    al-Biqāʿī and Ibn ʿĀshūr.

Do not simplify the relationship into a single formula. Identify both
continuity and differences.

### Pilot 3 --- Al-Qamar (54) → Ar-Raḥmān (55) → Al-Wāqiʿah (56)

Investigate the three-surah sequence as a research question, not a
predetermined conclusion.

**Al-Qamar (54):** - The nearness of the Hour and the moon-splitting
passage (54:1--2). - Repeated accounts of communities that rejected
warnings. - The recurring refrain *fa-kayfa kāna ʿadhābī wa-nudhuri* and
its function. - The closing movement concerning the righteous.

**Ar-Raḥmān (55):** - The opening sequence: teaching the Qur'an,
creation of humanity, speech, cosmic order and balance. - The repeated
refrain *fa-bi-ayyi ālāʾi rabbikumā tukadhdhibān*. - The address to both
jinn and humankind. - The movement through signs, accountability, the
two gardens and their descriptions.

**Al-Wāqiʿah (56):** - The certainty and impact of the Event. - The
three groups: the foremost, the people of the right and the people of
the left. - Resurrection, provision, creation, death and the Qur'an's
status. - The concluding glorification in 56:96.

Compare the sequence carefully: - Warning and rejected messengers in
Al-Qamar. - Divine signs, blessings, balance and accountability in
Ar-Raḥmān. - The Event and differentiated human outcomes in Al-Wāqiʿah.

Test whether this is a defensible thematic progression and locate
classical discussion of the transitions. Do not assert that the three
surahs form a formally established triptych unless evidence is found.

Investigate specific cross-links: - The Hour and resurrection in
54:1--8, 55:26--27 and 56:1--56. - Divine blessings, provision and
gratitude across the three surahs. - The repeated rhetorical refrains
and their distinct functions. - The fate of earlier rejecting
communities compared with the final human categories. - The closing ayah
of Al-Wāqiʿah (56:96) and the opening of Al-Ḥadīd (57:1) as a
neighbouring-surah transition. This is an adjacent link worth checking
in classical sources.

### Pilot 4 --- Opening/closing correspondence

Select a small group of surahs for which classical sources explicitly
discuss the relationship between their beginnings and endings. Use
al-Suyūṭī's *Marāṣid al-maṭāliʿ* and relevant discussion in *Al-Itqān*
as leads. Verify the actual text and citation before publication.

## 5. Research workflow

### Phase 0 --- Research infrastructure

1.  Create a source register with bibliographic metadata and
    source-quality labels.
2.  Create a canonical Qur'an text dataset with verified Arabic,
    surah/ayah identifiers and a documented text source.
3.  Select one or more reliable translations; record translator, edition
    and licensing.
4.  Create a consistent transliteration and Arabic typography policy.
5.  Build a citation model that can represent work, author, edition,
    volume, page, chapter, URL and access date.
6.  Define the relationship schema and confidence labels before
    producing large-scale content.
7.  Create a review queue for claims with missing citations, disputed
    interpretations or low confidence.

### Phase 1 --- Source discovery and verification

For every research topic:

1.  Search primary classical sources first.
2.  Locate the actual passage in the source, not just a search-engine
    snippet.
3.  Capture exact bibliographic details.
4.  Verify Arabic quotations against the source.
5.  Translate or paraphrase carefully; preserve the original Arabic
    quotation where permitted and useful.
6.  Compare at least two relevant tafsir works for major interpretive
    claims where feasible.
7.  Record disagreements, silence and uncertainty.
8.  Keep a research log with search terms, sources checked and
    unresolved questions.

Never cite a book merely because it is in the source list. The citation
must support the specific claim.

### Phase 2 --- Surah-by-surah structural research

For all 114 surahs:

1.  Produce a surah overview.
2.  Analyse the opening and closing.
3.  Propose internal sections with verse ranges.
4.  Identify transitions and rhetorical features.
5.  Compare the surah with its immediate neighbours.
6.  Map significant cross-Qur'anic themes.
7.  Attach sources and confidence labels.
8.  Submit each surah dossier for review before marking it complete.

### Phase 3 --- Relationship research

Create dedicated relationship studies for:

-   All 113 adjacent-surah transitions.
-   Important opening-to-ending correspondences.
-   Major non-adjacent thematic links.
-   Repeated narratives and their different contexts.
-   Repeated expressions, refrains and rhetorical patterns.

Prioritise evidence and significance, not the number of links. A smaller
set of well-supported connections is better than a huge graph of weak
associations.

### Phase 4 --- Synthesis and peer review

-   Compare findings across tafsir sources.
-   Check that verse references and Arabic text are correct.
-   Review claims about canonical order and revelation chronology.
-   Challenge circular reasoning (e.g. using a proposed structure as
    proof of the same structure).
-   Ask a qualified Sunni Qur'an teacher/scholar to review sensitive or
    contested religious interpretations before presenting them as
    authoritative.
-   Maintain a visible correction history.

### Phase 5 --- Website publication

Publish only records that meet the minimum citation and review
requirements. Clearly mark incomplete research and hypotheses.

## 7. Gemini ecosystem agent design

Design the workflow as a set of specialised agents or stages. Use the
available Gemini ecosystem tools and models, but keep the process
model-agnostic so the project can later migrate to local/open-source
models.

### Agent A --- Research Planner

-   Turns a research question into bounded sub-questions.
-   Identifies relevant surahs, ayat, themes and likely classical
    sources.
-   Produces a research plan and a list of claims that need
    verification.
-   Does not write conclusions before evidence collection.

### Agent B --- Qur'an Text Researcher

-   Retrieves exact Arabic passages and verse references from the
    canonical dataset.
-   Finds repeated wording, roots, phrases and related passages.
-   Checks all verse references.
-   Never relies on generated Arabic from model memory.

### Agent C --- Classical Tafsir Researcher

-   Searches the selected Sunni tafsir corpus.
-   Extracts relevant explanations with exact bibliographic references.
-   Distinguishes direct statements from implied observations.
-   Reports when a source does not discuss the proposed relationship.

### Agent D --- Munāsabāt Analyst

-   Investigates relationships between adjacent surahs, ayat, openings
    and endings.
-   Compares classical munāsabāt discussions with the actual Qur'anic
    text.
-   Labels new observations as hypotheses.
-   Includes alternative explanations and counter-evidence.

### Agent E --- Surah Structure Analyst

-   Proposes internal verse groupings.
-   Analyses transitions, discourse, repeated expressions, contrasts and
    parallel structures.
-   Provides evidence for each proposed boundary.
-   Shows competing structures where credible scholarship differs.

### Agent F --- Cross-Surah Theme Analyst

-   Builds thematic maps across the Qur'an.
-   Separates shared terms from shared concepts.
-   Tracks narrative variations and context.
-   Avoids flattening distinct passages into one generic message.

### Agent G --- Citation & Fact Checker

-   Checks every verse reference against the canonical dataset.
-   Checks source metadata and citation completeness.
-   Flags quotations that cannot be verified.
-   Detects unsupported claims, overconfident language and invented
    references.
-   Blocks publication when critical references are missing.

### Agent H --- Sunni Scholarly Review Assistant

-   Produces a concise review dossier for qualified human review.
-   Highlights doctrinally sensitive claims, disputed interpretations
    and weak evidence.
-   Does not impersonate a scholar or issue fatwas.
-   Human review is required for claims presented as authoritative where
    scholarly judgement is material.

### Agent I --- Editorial & Website Publisher

-   Converts approved research into clear, readable website content.
-   Preserves citations, confidence labels and caveats.
-   Produces diagrams and cross-links from structured data.
-   Cannot upgrade a hypothesis to an established conclusion.

### Orchestration requirements

-   Each agent must have a defined input/output schema.
-   Preserve source references between agents.
-   Use a durable research ledger so work can resume after interruption.
-   Make every stage idempotent: rerunning it should update or version
    records rather than duplicate them.
-   Keep raw source evidence, extracted notes, model analysis and
    approved publication content separate.
-   Store model name, prompt version, run date and review status for
    every generated research record.
-   Require the fact-check stage before publication.
-   Do not allow an agent to invent a citation to fill a missing field.

## 8. Website experience and visual direction

The website should feel like a refined digital Qur'anic research atlas:
calm, contemplative, scholarly and easy to explore. Avoid a generic
admin dashboard aesthetic.

### Main navigation

1.  **Explore the Qur'an** --- all 114 surahs in muṣḥaf order.
2.  **Surah Connections** --- previous/next surah relationships.
3.  **Ayah Connections** --- cross-references and related passages.
4.  **Surah Architecture** --- internal sections and structure.
5.  **Themes** --- thematic networks across the Qur'an.
6.  **Research Library** --- tafsir sources, ʿulūm al-Qur'an and
    methodology.
7.  **About the Method** --- evidence labels, limitations and review
    process.

### Surah detail page

Each surah page should include:

-   Surah name in Arabic and English, number and classification.
-   A concise overview.
-   A visual section map with clickable verse ranges.
-   Opening and ending analysis.
-   Previous and next surah connections.
-   Important cross-surah links.
-   Key themes and recurring expressions.
-   Classical tafsir insights with citations.
-   Research observations clearly separated from sourced explanations.
-   A bibliography and last-reviewed date.

### Relationship detail page

For every connection show:

-   The two or more passages being compared.
-   Arabic text and selected translation.
-   Exact verse references.
-   The type of relationship.
-   A plain-language explanation.
-   Classical source evidence.
-   Counterpoints or alternative explanations.
-   Confidence label and review status.
-   Links to the full surah pages.

### Visualisations

Build useful, accessible visualisations:

-   **Surah sequence map:** 114 surahs in canonical order, with selected
    links between neighbours.
-   **Relationship graph:** nodes for surahs, ayat and themes; edges
    labelled by relationship type.
-   **Surah structure map:** verse ranges displayed as sequential
    sections.
-   **Theme explorer:** select a theme and explore its passages across
    the Qur'an.
-   **Passage comparison:** side-by-side passages with shared terms
    highlighted.
-   **Opening/ending view:** compare the beginning and ending of a
    surah.
-   **Progression view:** trace a theme through selected surahs.

Visual links must be clickable and explainable. Do not use colour alone
to communicate confidence or relationship type. Provide text labels and
a legend. Avoid a graph so dense that it becomes decorative noise.

### Design system

-   Elegant Arabic typography with correct right-to-left rendering.
-   Clear English typography and generous whitespace.
-   Restrained palette inspired by manuscript ink, warm paper and subtle
    green/teal accents.
-   Strong contrast and responsive layouts.
-   Mobile-first reading experience.
-   Dark mode optional.
-   Respect reduced-motion settings.
-   No autoplay audio or distracting animation.
-   Keep the Qur'anic Arabic readable and never distort or decorate the
    text itself.

### Technical preferences

Prefer a lightweight, maintainable architecture:

-   Semantic HTML, modern CSS and vanilla JavaScript where practical.
-   Pico.css or similarly lightweight CSS may be used if it helps; do
    not introduce a large frontend framework without a clear need.
-   Static-site generation or a static site backed by JSON is preferred
    for low-cost hosting.
-   Host on GitHub Pages or another free static host if project needs
    permit.
-   Store research data in JSON initially; keep the schema suitable for
    SQLite later.
-   No mandatory paid API at runtime.
-   Gemini is a research/build-time tool, not a requirement for every
    website visitor.
-   Keep Arabic text and approved research content available offline
    where practical.
-   Make search fast and provide filters for surah, ayah, theme,
    relationship type and confidence.

## 9. Quality assurance and acceptance criteria

A research item is not publishable unless:

-   Every Qur'anic reference resolves to the correct surah and ayah.
-   Arabic text is checked against the canonical dataset.
-   Every tafsir claim has a traceable source reference.
-   Direct quotations have been verified against the cited edition.
-   Paraphrases are not presented as quotations.
-   Classical scholarly claims and project hypotheses are clearly
    separated.
-   Disagreements and limitations are visible.
-   No unsupported claim about the reason for canonical placement is
    presented as fact.
-   The content has passed automated checks and the required human
    review.

Website acceptance:

-   All 114 surahs can be browsed.
-   A user can move from a surah to its previous and next surah.
-   A user can open a relationship and see the exact passages and
    supporting evidence.
-   Theme pages show relevant passages with context, not just keyword
    matches.
-   Every visualised edge opens an explanation and its sources.
-   Arabic and English layouts work on desktop and mobile.
-   Search and filters work without a Gemini API call.
-   The website clearly explains its methodology and limitations.

## 12. Seed bibliography and research leads

These are starting points, not substitutes for verifying the relevant
passages in the original works.

### Classical Sunni works to acquire and consult

1.  Al-Ṭabarī, *Jāmiʿ al-bayān ʿan taʾwīl āy al-Qur'ān*.
2.  Al-Baghawī, *Maʿālim al-tanzīl*.
3.  Ibn Kathīr, *Tafsīr al-Qur'ān al-ʿaẓīm*.
4.  Al-Qurṭubī, *Al-Jāmiʿ li-aḥkām al-Qur'ān*.
5.  Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb*.
6.  Ibn ʿĀshūr, *Al-Taḥrīr wa al-tanwīr*.
7.  Al-Zarkashī, *Al-Burhān fī ʿulūm al-Qur'ān*.
8.  Al-Suyūṭī, *Al-Itqān fī ʿulūm al-Qur'ān*.
9.  Al-Suyūṭī, *Marāṣid al-maṭāliʿ fī tanāsub al-maqāṭiʿ wa al-maṭāliʿ*.
10. Al-Biqāʿī, *Naẓm al-durar fī tanāsub al-āyāt wa al-suwar*.
11. Ibn al-Zubayr al-Gharnāṭī, *Al-Burhān fī tartīb suwar al-Qur'ān*.

### Methodological and academic leads

-   The *Oxford Handbook of Qur'anic Studies*, especially its discussion
    of Qur'anic syntax, munāsabah and the historical development of
    coherence analysis.
-   Research on surah structure and structural dividers, including the
    work of Neal Robinson, A.H. Mathias Zahniser, Mustansir Mir and
    Angelika Neuwirth. Treat these as academic approaches, not as
    replacements for Sunni tafsir.
-   Studies of the classical science of munāsabāt and the works of
    al-Zarkashī, al-Suyūṭī, Ibn al-Zubayr and al-Biqāʿī.

## 13. Final instruction to the Gemini research team

Work in small, auditable research units. Start with the four pilots. Do
not attempt to generate all 114 surah structures and hundreds of
relationships in one model run.

For every conclusion, answer these questions:

1.  What exact Qur'anic passages are being connected?
2.  What is the relationship being proposed?
3.  Which recognised Sunni sources discuss it?
4.  What does the Arabic text and immediate context show?
5.  Is this a classical explanation, a textual observation or a new
    hypothesis?
6.  What alternative reading or limitation should the reader know?
7.  Can another researcher reproduce the path from source to conclusion?

The finished website should help readers see the Qur'an's rich
interconnections while remaining faithful to the text, respectful of the
tafsir tradition, honest about scholarly differences and transparent
about the limits of every claim.

**Success means traceable understanding, not the largest number of
connections.**
